# Program 4: Heat-maps & Min-Max Algorithm

## 4(a): Heat-map
**What it does:** Uses a color scale to represent data values in a matrix or table format. In ML, it is mostly used for **Correlation Matrices**.
**What to look for:** Dark red or dark blue squares. A high correlation (near 1 or -1) means two features are highly related. A correlation near 0 means they have no relationship.
**Real-World Example:** Thermal cameras showing heat loss in a house, or a stock market tracker where green boxes mean a stock went up and red means it went down.

## 4(b): Min-Max Algorithm
**Logic / Algorithm:** A decision-making algorithm used in game theory for two-player, zero-sum games. It assumes the opponent plays optimally. The "Maximizer" tries to get the highest score possible, while the "Minimizer" tries to force the lowest score possible. It builds a game tree and works backwards from the end states.
**Real-World Example:** Building a Tic-Tac-Toe or Chess AI. The AI (Maximizer) looks 5 moves ahead and chooses the move where the opponent's best counter-attack still leaves the AI in a strong position.
